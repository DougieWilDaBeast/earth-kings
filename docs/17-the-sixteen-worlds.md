# 17 — The Fall and the Sixteen Worlds

The shape of the whole game, as the founders agreed it in
[joint session 1](worldbuilding/voice-notes/2026-09-24-joint-session-1.md) on 2026-09-24. This is
**direction, not the build.** Almost none of it exists in code yet; what exists and what does not is
marked at the end, and the work is on the [roadmap](05-roadmap.md). The rules it sets are
[D31–D40](06-decisions.md); the answers it rests on, word for word, are in
[answers.md](worldbuilding/answers.md) under _Joint session 1_.

Where a detail is still open it says so and points at the
[agenda](worldbuilding/answers.md#what-is-still-to-decide).

## The fall

Sixteen people live above the world, in a heavenly place, at godhood — **demigods**, not gods.
Something goes wrong up there. All sixteen are thrown down to **Earth**.

- **They lose their memories** in the fall.
- **They land anywhere** — a castle, a forest, a city, a bad part of the country or a good one. Where
  each one lands is randomised per world.
- **They look exactly as they did above**, and pass for ordinary people — except for **one mark all
  sixteen share**, in the same place on each of them (a tattoo or glowing eyes; not yet chosen). The
  mark is how they recognise one another.
- **The moment they land, the multiverse splits into sixteen.** Each world is a copy of the one they
  fell towards, and each belongs to one of the sixteen. In your world, the other fifteen are
  **copies**; the originals are in their own worlds.

Why the disaster happened, and who caused it, is found out at the end.

## One of the sixteen is you

The player is one of the sixteen. The intro shows the fall, so **the player knows what the lead has
forgotten** — the game opens on dramatic irony.

- The lead is picked by the four-question temper quiz, with every one of the sixteen reachable by
  choosing ([D29](06-decisions.md)). Which of the two is the front door is open — agenda item 7.
- Choosing a lead switches **that** character's own behaviour off. **The other fifteen keep theirs**
  and live their own lives, driven by their temper: they go where they go, join what they join, and
  you meet them mid-story, if you meet them at all.
- You can team up with any of them. You never have to, and some of them are no good in a party.
- Each of the sixteen has **their own line of quests**, and every line ends at the Tower. The lead
  climbs without knowing what the climb is for.
- What the lead was before the fall comes back in pieces — milestones, conversations — and collects
  into a background the player can read back.

## The Tower

There is **one Tower in each world, in the middle of it**. Sixteen worlds, sixteen Towers.

- **The Tower tests the climber.** It is a proving ground: can this person defend Earth against the
  other worlds? Strong people climb it for the honour of it, and other people in the world are
  climbing it too, their own way.
- **Only your party goes in.**
- **Every floor you clear sends you back out** into the world, and the world moves on.
- **The climb comes in chapters.** Every five floors the world changes — its story advances, things
  that were brewing happen — and **the next chapter needs something from the world**: an item, a
  gate cleared, a quest done. Ignore the world and you reach each chapter weaker, into a harder
  world, with experience left lying about.
- **At the end of every chapter the Tower shows the census**: how many of the sixteen are still
  alive, across all the worlds.

**How many floors** is open: ten ship today, a hundred was floated, and whether a hundred could be
fun was the session's main worry. Agenda item 1. **Whether anything moves the world between
chapters** — the step clock that runs today — is agenda item 2.

## Gates

**A gate tests the world.** It is something happening _to_ the world, and the world has to find a way
to stop it.

- **Inside is a whole other world**, any size — an ice country of ice elves, a desert of scorpion
  things — with **an objective** to complete, sometimes against the clock.
- **You cannot leave a gate until it is beaten.**
- **Anyone can go in**, and in any number. The worst gates are fought by crowds.
- **Ranked E to S.** An **S-rank gate is a national threat**: kingdoms and armies gather and wait
  until there are enough strong people to go in together.
- **The Adventurers Guild** manages gates and cares about them rather than the Tower. Royalty cares
  about gates too. Strong individuals care about the Tower.
- Gates hold **bosses**, and gate rewards are what open the Tower's next chapter.

## When the lead dies

**Their world ends.** The camera pulls up over the whole of it and watches it be destroyed — the
lead was the one who was supposed to save it. **It never comes back.**

Then the player **becomes another of the sixteen, in that one's own world**, chosen from whoever is
still standing.

- **Everything starts again at level 1.** No levels, gear or floors carry. This was argued in the
  session and settled that way — see the [divergence ledger](worldbuilding/divergence-ledger.md).
- **The worlds are siblings.** They split from one moment, so they tend to play out alike: where
  someone crash-landed or where an item lies is **knowledge worth carrying**. How alike is a tuning
  question.
- **Sixteen worlds is sixteen chances.** Lose all sixteen and there is nobody left to fight.

### What does carry

1. **What the player knows.**
2. **The dead lead, as a ghost.** Meditate at a campfire to meet them. They can be asked how they
   failed, what they did well, and they hand out quests from what they found. What they know is
   **the journal of the run they died in**, saved at the moment of death — quests taken, places
   found, people met. How much they share depends on their temper and on how much you have earned
   their trust. The living sixteen are visible at the fire too, and cannot be spoken to until they
   die.
3. **The dead lead's signature weapon.** Every one of the sixteen has a weapon made for them. When
   they die it **crash-lands somewhere in the next world** — god-tier, but nobody is proficient with
   it, and somebody else may have found it first. Rumours lead to it. **Finding it is what opens the
   ghost fully.** Where it lands the first time — easy to find, or random — is agenda item 12.

**Nothing arrives early.** The ghost's news and the weapon only show up once the new lead has got as
far as the old one did. On the first run the census says sixteen alive at every chapter. If the first
lead died on floor ten, the second lead's census says sixteen at floor five and **fifteen at floor
ten**.

Whether the first lead is _also_ alive as a copy in the second world is agenda item 5.

## Past the top

Topping the Tower is **not the end of the game**. It pulls the lead up to the level of the universes,
where **time has stopped** for everyone who finished first, so nobody is kept waiting.

- There the lead meets **the other surviving leads — the originals — each at the most powerful they
  could have become**, even if that character died young in your world.
- Only **the worlds still standing** send someone. How many of the sixteen reached the top changes the
  whole endgame: who fights, and how.
- **Everyone there knows that only one world is going to be left standing.** The winner is the Earth
  King.
- Above them are **the real gods**, and a force that has arranged for one world to survive. The last
  thing the game asks is how to stop it.

How the contest itself is fought — duels, a bracket, alliances — is open.

## Names

The sixteen carry **fixed, god-style names in a Roman and Greek register**, the same every run.
Examples spoken in the session: Cassius, Caesar, Bellona, Ares.

Everyone else's names are **regional**. Near an imperial centre the nobility carry strong, eccentric
forms; ordinary people farther out carry **shortened stems** of them. Everyone has **a real name and
an earned one** — a nickname or title won by deeds, like _Goblin Hunter Caesar_ — and fame travels by
the earned one.

## Tone

**Dark fantasy, getting darker as it goes.** Not repetitive, not all laughs, not held up by dialogue.
The fight is the thing that has to be right — see [18 — Combat direction](18-combat-direction.md).

## What exists today, and what does not

| Piece | In the build |
| --- | --- |
| Sixteen temper slots, the quiz, the picker | Yes — [13](13-heroes-and-tempers.md). No character of the sixteen is written yet |
| The run ending when the lead falls | Yes — `D14`. It ends the run, not a world |
| One Tower, floors, a floor-10 fight | Yes — ten floors, [D16](06-decisions.md), [D25](06-decisions.md). No chapters, keys, census or return-to-world per floor |
| Gates, ranks E–S, delves | Yes. A party can retreat from a delve today; the new rule says it cannot |
| The fall, the mark, random crash sites | No |
| The fifteen living their own lives | No — non-leads stand in fixed places. The "NPC society" seam in [01](01-vision.md) is where it goes |
| Sibling worlds, the world ending, the next lead | No. `World` serialises and is seeded ([D09](06-decisions.md)), which is the seam for a sibling world |
| Ghosts, the journal, signature weapons | No. The journal screen and `Annals` already record what a run did |
| The contest past the top | No |
| Regional names and earned titles | No. `Renown` already tracks standing per place, which is where a title would come from |
