# 17 — The Fall and the Sixteen Worlds

The shape of the whole game, as the founders agreed it in
[joint session 1](worldbuilding/voice-notes/2026-09-24-joint-session-1.md) on 2026-09-24. This is
**direction, not the build.** Almost none of it exists in code yet; what exists and what does not is
marked at the end, and the work is on the [roadmap](05-roadmap.md). The rules it sets are
[D31–D40](06-decisions.md); the answers it rests on, word for word, are in
[answers.md](worldbuilding/answers.md) under _Joint session 1_.

Where a detail is still open it says so and points at the
[agenda](worldbuilding/answers.md#what-is-still-to-decide).

## The fall

Sixteen people live above the world, in a heavenly place, at godhood — **demigods**, not gods.
Something goes wrong up there. All sixteen are thrown down to **Earth**.

- **They lose their memories** in the fall.
- **They land anywhere** — a castle, a forest, a city, a bad part of the country or a good one. Where
  each one lands is randomised per world.
- **They look exactly as they did above**, and pass for ordinary people — except for **one mark all
  sixteen share**, in the same place on each of them (a tattoo or glowing eyes; not yet chosen). The
  mark is how they recognise one another.
- **The moment they land, the multiverse splits into sixteen.** Each world is a copy of the one they
  fell towards, and each belongs to one of the sixteen. In your world, the other fifteen are
  **copies**; the originals are in their own worlds.

Why the disaster happened, and who caused it, is found out at the end.

**What the copies really are** ([D440](06-decisions.md), 2026-10-24, `dougie`): not copies. The sixteen worlds are
one land split apart, like Pangaea, and beating floor 100 remerges them. Everyone believes in copies until then.
Planned, nothing built: [the ending frame](worldbuilding/the-ending-frame.md).

## One of the sixteen is you

The player is one of the sixteen. The intro shows the fall, so **the player knows what the lead has
forgotten** — the game opens on dramatic irony.

- The lead is picked by the four-question temper quiz. **The quiz is the front door**
  ([D44](06-decisions.md)): once it is answered the player can answer again, as often as they like,
  or open the roster of all sixteen and choose.
- Choosing a lead switches **that** character's own behaviour off. **The other fifteen keep theirs**
  and live their own lives, driven by their temper: they go where they go, join what they join, and
  you meet them mid-story, if you meet them at all.
- You can team up with any of them. You never have to, and some of them are no good in a party.
- Each of the sixteen has **their own line of quests**, and every line ends at the Tower. The lead
  climbs without knowing what the climb is for.
- What the lead was before the fall comes back in pieces. **Every chapter of the Tower returns
  memories** of their own life and their part in the story of the Earth Kings, and the whole story
  comes together only at the end. Collecting every fragment plays it as a cinematic, the way _Until
  Dawn_'s totems do ([D53](06-decisions.md)).
- **Every lead carries one standing quest from the moment they land: "Ascend to heaven"**, until it
  is done.

## The Tower

There is **one Tower in each world, in the middle of it**. Sixteen worlds, sixteen Towers.

- **The Tower tests the climber.** It is a proving ground: can this person defend Earth against the
  other worlds? Strong people climb it for the honour of it, and other people in the world are
  climbing it too, their own way.
- **Only your party goes in.**
- **Every floor you clear sends you back out** into the world, and the world moves on.
- **The climb comes in chapters.** Every five floors the world changes — its story advances, things
  that were brewing happen — and **the next chapter needs something from the world**: an item, a
  gate cleared, a quest done. Ignore the world and you reach each chapter weaker, into a harder
  world, with experience left lying about.
- **At the end of every chapter the Tower shows the census**: how many of the sixteen are still
  alive, across all the worlds.

**A hundred floors, twenty chapters of five** ([D48](06-decisions.md), settled in joint session 2:
"because it's just iconic"). Chapters may grow longer later, and if they do the story is made to fit.
**Nothing runs on steps**: the world moves on at milestones, with the Tower's chapters among them, and
nothing is against the clock ([D49](06-decisions.md)). The build still runs a step clock until M18.

## Gates

**A gate tests the world.** It is something happening _to_ the world, and the world has to find a way
to stop it.

- **Inside is a whole other world**, any size — an ice country of ice elves, a desert of scorpion
  things — with **an objective** to complete, sometimes against the clock.
- **You cannot leave a gate until it is beaten.**
- **Anyone can go in**, and in any number. The worst gates are fought by crowds.
- **Ranked E to S.** An **S-rank gate is a national threat**: kingdoms and armies gather and wait
  until there are enough strong people to go in together.
- **The Adventurers Guild** manages gates and cares about them rather than the Tower. Royalty cares
  about gates too. Strong individuals care about the Tower.
- Gates hold **bosses**, and gate rewards are what open the Tower's next chapter.

## When the lead dies

**There are two ways to play it** ([D51](06-decisions.md)). In **Classic**, saves can be reloaded:
when the lead dies the game asks whether to reload, or to **ascend to heaven** and choose the next
character. In **Iron Man** there is one life, and the lead's death ascends them and goes straight
back to choosing. Either way, once they have ascended:

**Their world ends.** The camera pulls up over the whole of it and watches it be destroyed — the
lead was the one who was supposed to save it. **It never comes back.**

Then the player **becomes another of the sixteen, in that one's own world**, chosen from whoever is
still standing.

- **The four questions are asked again, and the dead cannot be answered into** ([D43](06-decisions.md)).
  An answer is removed once every character it could still lead to has died. If the first lead was
  INTJ, the second quiz goes Inward, Sky-read, Cold-eyed and then offers only _Loose_. If INTP is dead
  as well, _Cold-eyed_ is gone one question earlier. The picker offers only the living.
- **A question left with one answer is still asked** ([D44](06-decisions.md)). The player has to
  pick the one answer that is left, so that after every death they see an option has gone.
- **Everything starts again at level 1.** No levels, gear or floors carry. This was argued in the
  session and settled that way — see the [divergence ledger](worldbuilding/divergence-ledger.md).
- **The worlds are siblings.** They split from one moment, so they tend to play out alike: where
  someone crash-landed or where an item lies is **knowledge worth carrying**. How alike is a tuning
  question.
- **Sixteen worlds is sixteen chances.** Lose all sixteen and there is nobody left to fight.

### What does carry

1. **What the player knows.**
2. **The dead lead, as a ghost.** Meditate at a campfire to meet them. They can be asked how they
   failed, what they did well, and they hand out quests from what they found. What they know is
   **the journal of the run they died in**, saved at the moment of death — quests taken, places
   found, people met. How much they share depends on their temper and on how much you have earned
   their trust. The living sixteen are visible at the fire too, and cannot be spoken to until they
   die.
3. **The dead lead's signature weapon.** Every one of the sixteen has a weapon made for them. When
   they die it **crash-lands somewhere in the next world** — god-tier, but nobody is proficient with
   it, and somebody else may have found it first: half the time a band of the land stands over the crater
   and has to be beaten before it can be picked up ([D311](06-decisions.md)). Rumours lead to it. **Finding it is what opens the
   ghost fully.** Where it lands the first time: **anywhere the company can walk**, found by rumour (`doug-md`,
   2026-10-12, [D60](06-decisions.md); built; waits on `doug-md`).

**Nothing arrives early.** The ghost's news and the weapon only show up once the new lead has got as
far as the old one did. On the first run the census says sixteen alive at every chapter. If the first
lead died on floor ten, the second lead's census says sixteen at floor five and **fifteen at floor
ten**.

**The first lead is alive in the second world as well** ([D52](06-decisions.md)): an ordinary NPC
built from their stats, with the personality they started with, who can be met and talked to like
anyone else. The fifteen in any world are copies, and this is one of them.

## Past the top

Topping the Tower is **not the end of the game**. It pulls the lead up to the level of the universes,
where **time has stopped** for everyone who finished first, so nobody is kept waiting.

- There the lead meets **the other surviving leads — the originals — each at the most powerful they
  could have become**, even if that character died young in your world.
- Only **the worlds still standing** send someone. How many of the sixteen reached the top changes the
  whole endgame: who fights, and how.
- **Everyone there knows that only one world is going to be left standing.** The winner is the Earth
  King.
- Above them are **the real gods**, and a force that has arranged for one world to survive. The last
  thing the game asks is how to stop it.

How the contest itself is fought — duels, a bracket, alliances — is open.

**After it, the worlds remerge** ([D440](06-decisions.md)): the one world left standing is all sixteen, made one again.
The fallen leads are there as the demon kings, knowing every life they had and powerless to change; the living
merge aware of theirs. Then the last act ([D441](06-decisions.md)): the player chooses a side, Earth Kings or demon
kings, for the rest of the game. Planned, design only: [the ending frame](worldbuilding/the-ending-frame.md).

## Names

The sixteen carry **fixed, god-style names in a Roman and Greek register**, the same every run.
Examples spoken in the session: Cassius, Caesar, Bellona, Ares.

Everyone else's names are **regional**. Near an imperial centre the nobility carry strong, eccentric
forms; ordinary people farther out carry **shortened stems** of them. Everyone has **a real name and
an earned one** — a nickname or title won by deeds, like _Goblin Hunter Caesar_ — and fame travels by
the earned one.

## Tone

**Dark fantasy, getting darker as it goes.** Not repetitive, not all laughs, not held up by dialogue.
The fight is the thing that has to be right — see [18 — Combat direction](18-combat-direction.md).

## The others, as built

Built 2026-10-12 for M13 (`src/chronicle/fall.gd`, `src/chronicle/wanderers.gd`, `data/fall.json`).
Everything here is **Inferred** — agenda items 25 to 29 in
[answers.md](worldbuilding/answers.md#what-is-still-to-decide) — and goes back to the founders.

- **Landing.** A new run draws the lead's crash site from the world seed: open ground joined by land
  to the Tower, well away from it and from open gates, within a day's walk of somewhere with a roof.
  The crater becomes Home ("Where You Fell"): the bed upgrades, the safety and the rest all work
  there. The first thing the run shows is a short landing beat, and the lead's standing quest.
- **Ascend to heaven.** The journal's _Quests & the Fallen_ page carries it at the top, with the
  Tower's floor and chapter, above the errands taken. The Tower wears an ASCEND mark on the map.
- **The others.** The sixteen ([D78](06-decisions.md)): whoever the run starts with is in the party;
  the other fifteen crash-land at their own seeded sites and walk straight for the nearest place
  of their own kind, the one [21](21-the-sixteen-first-draft.md#the-sixteen-at-a-glance) gives them
  "when someone else is the lead": Atlas a library, Janus and Pluto a gate, Bellona, Cato and Ares
  a keep, Cassius, Saturn, Caesar, Iris, Ceres and Juno a village, Vesta, Ultor, Diana and Liber a
  hut in open country. They stay there three times as long as anywhere else and go back to it
  most, and otherwise walk by temper — NT to libraries and the Tower, NF to libraries, villages and
  huts, SJ to keeps and villages, SP to gates and keeps. Iris and Liber never stay long anywhere.
  **Inferred**, agenda item 69. The twelve who stood in before are no longer leads
  ([D79](06-decisions.md)): each stands, for good, in the place their story belongs to
  ([02](02-design.md#the-original-twelve-met-in-the-world)).
- **Finding them.** Asking an inn for news, or resting at a settlement, can bring word of one, and
  marks where they were heard of. Standing where one stands shows them, and the mark. Both are
  drawn on the map, a rumour faintly.
- **Meeting them.** **X** on the map, or walking up to them inside the place, starts a short
  conversation, opening in their own words the first time and then in their temper's voice. An Outward temper will join on the first meeting, an Inward
  one only on the second, at the lead's level and only into a free seat in the party (four to start,
  up to six as the Tower is climbed).

## Sixteen worlds, as built

Built 2026-10-12 for M15 (`src/chronicle/siblings.gd`, `src/world/world_end.gd`,
`data/fall.json` → `siblings`). Everything here is **Inferred** — agenda items 50 to 56 in
[answers.md](worldbuilding/answers.md#what-is-still-to-decide) — and goes back to the founders.

- **The end.** Once a lead who died has ascended (Classic) or straight from the fall (Iron Man),
  the map is shown one last time: the camera rises over the whole of it while it burns out from
  the Tower to the sea, and the lead's name is said over it. Any key looks away. The world is kept
  in the record of the fallen, marked gone, with what the lead was, how far they got, and the
  journal of the run: a line for every gate shut, person met, place stood in and enemy learned.
  Then the run is told, and the next lead is chosen from the living.
- **The sibling.** With no seed typed on the picker, the next run is built on the first world's
  seed: the same ground, places, names and crash sites. A quarter of the gates' state and the
  libraries' books are dealt again, and the world's dice start somewhere new. The new lead is
  level 1 with nothing carried over. A typed seed is a world of its own, and nothing comes through.
- **The earlier lead, alive.** Their copy waits at the settlement nearest where the new lead fell,
  and the landing beat says the name is being talked about there. They are met like the others, and
  answer "Have we met before?" in their temper's voice. If they join, it is at the new lead's level,
  with the calling and beliefs they started with.
- **Nothing early.** Once the new lead has the old lead's level and has climbed to their floor,
  the old lead's weapon falls out of a clear sky (the company sees it, and which way), their ghost
  comes to the fire, and the census at the end of a chapter counts them out.
- **The weapon.** Each stand-in has one (`equipment.json`, `signature`): god-tier and suited to
  nobody. It lands on open ground 5 to 12 tiles from where the new lead fell (agenda item 12's "easy
  to find", item 53). Inns and rests point at it, which marks it on the map; walking onto the crater
  takes it.
- **The ghost.** At any fire the company sits at — the camp after a rest, a hut, home — in a free
  seat, the fire showing through them. They answer how they fell, what went well, what they found
  out there and where their weapon is. What they found is the journal, shared a line or three at
  first by temper, two more every night after, and all of it once their weapon is found.
- **The dream** ([D249](06-decisions.md), **Inferred**). The world that ends keeps six of its lead's
  weightiest deeds (a gate shut, a town held or burned, someone pulled off the road). In the worlds
  after, somebody to talk to at a place within a tile of one has dreamt it, and says so once before
  their hello: "I dreamt of someone with that mark on them. Pluto, they were called. They shut
  Greyhollow."
- **The journal page** (_Quests & the Fallen_) says which world of the sixteen this is and what has
  come through from each one that ended.
