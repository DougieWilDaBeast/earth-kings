# 16.15 — One Piece

**Co-founder's list** · Japanese manga, Eiichiro Oda · **Brief as given:** *world building, size of world, different forms of travel, depth in world*
**Serves:** `LN1`–`LN10`, `SK9`, `TG4`, `EC5`, `HS1`–`HS9`, `NW1`–`NW5`, `AR7`, `FR8`, `GT6`, `MX8`, `CO4`
**Checked against:** One Piece Fandom (Grand Line, Blue Planet, World)

## What it is

A water world with **one continent**, the Red Line, and over twenty million islands. The Grand Line
runs around the planet, walled on both sides by the **Calm Belt** — bands with no wind, no current
and no weather, impossible to sail and full of Sea Kings — so the entire route has effectively **one
entrance**. Inside it, ordinary compasses fail, because **every island generates its own magnetic
field**. Navigation is done with a **Log Pose**, which locks onto one island's magnetism and, after
you have stayed there long enough, resets and points at the next. **How long it takes to set differs
per island** — hours on one, a full year on another. The second half of the route needs a
three-needle pose, because islands there appear and vanish.

And the two facts that make the world feel enormous: **nobody has a complete map**, and the planet
used to have far more land, which sank during a century the world's government has erased from the
record.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Navigation is a sequence, not a choice.** You go where the pose points, and the route is the story.
2. **Each destination costs its own amount of time.** Some places hold you for a week and some for a year, and that is a property of the place.
3. **One entrance.** The geography makes the journey irreversible without anyone enforcing it.
4. **Nobody has the map.** Cartography is an unfinished project and a character's life ambition.
5. **The erased century.** The world's biggest fact is a gap in the record that someone maintains.
6. **Travel diversifies as the world deepens** — sea, sky, undersea — and each is a different ruleset.

## Candidates

### `LN6` / `MX8` — how does travel between sites actually work?
**Candidate:** **each site holds you for its own length of time.** Arriving somewhere costs steps; *leaving* it costs a different number depending on where it is, and the number is a fact about the place, not the distance.
**Why it fits:** the repo already prices everything in steps and already chose an FFTA-style node map. This turns the node map from a menu into terrain, with no pathfinding and no walkable overworld. The cheapest possible way to make a node graph feel like a world.
**Costs:** small, and mostly data. It does make route planning legible in a way that invites optimisation — worth checking against `SM12`.

### `FR8` / `HS4` — the thing that is never explained
**Candidate:** **a stretch of history everyone can point at and nobody can read.** Not a mystery the game withholds — a gap the *world* maintains, with someone benefitting.
**Falls out:** `HS4` (is there a written history, and who keeps it) — yes, and it has a hole in it. `NW5` — the record is central, official and incomplete on purpose. `FA4` — the church's authority partly rests on the hole.
**Why it fits:** this is the strongest `FR8` candidate on either list, because it satisfies "never explained" without requiring the *designers* to know the answer either.

### `LN2` / `LN10` — how big is the world, and is the map the whole of it?
**Candidate:** **nobody has the map.** Not "the map is the frontier of a larger world" — the stronger version: the complete map does not exist, several partial ones disagree, and possessing a good one is wealth.
**Pairs with 16.13:** mana-density regions plus incomplete cartography gives you the whole `LN` block in two decisions.

### `GT6` / `SK9` — is there anywhere the gates don't reach?
**Candidate:** yes, and it is unreachable for an ordinary geographic reason rather than a magical one. A dead band. No one goes, and the reason is boring and absolute.
**Why it fits:** boring impossibilities are more convincing than warded ones, and it answers `BN2` (edges of the map) without a wall.

### `AR7` / `HS2` — ruins, and what the world used to be
**Candidate:** **there was more land.** The ruins aren't abandoned settlements, they're the high ground of somewhere that mostly isn't there any more.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **One Piece is 1,100+ chapters and the cosmology is still being revealed.** Recent revelations (sea levels, the planet's former configuration) are late-series and some are delivered by an unreliable in-world broadcast. Take them as *the shape of a reveal*, not as facts — the lesson is that a 25-year-old world can still have its biggest fact undelivered.
- **Log Pose mechanics have canon inconsistencies** that fans catalogue at length — set times, Eternal Poses, how the New World's three needles work. Irrelevant: the candidate above uses only the core idea, which is stable.
- **Scale is not reproducible.** Twenty million islands works because a weekly manga can spend twenty-five years. Earth Kings has ten factions and a submission deadline. Take **the feeling of an incomplete map**, which is cheap, not the island count, which is not.

## What not to take

Devil Fruits. A catalogue of unique named powers, each different, each authored, is the exact
opposite of a generative grammar with proficiency levels (`PW1`, `PW5`). It is also the most
imitated thing in the source and would drag `PW` straight into a different genre.
