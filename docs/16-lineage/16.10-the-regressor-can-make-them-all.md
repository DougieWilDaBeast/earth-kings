# 16.10 — The Regressor Can Make Them All

**Tier B** · Korean web novel / manhwa (*회귀자가 다 만들어줌*, also *The Regressor Creates Everything*) · **Serves:** `MK7`, `MK8`, `MK10`, `MK12`, `EC8`, `EC10`, `LP6`, `LP7`, `MX23`, `SM8`, `OT8`
**Checked against:** NovelUpdates, MangaUpdates, Wuxiaworld series pages

## What it is

Lee Se-Hoon is the **blacksmith** of humanity's last united force. The war ends in mutual
destruction, and at the end of it he regresses thirty-one years to his days as a candidate at
Babel, the hero training institution. His profession is not a generic crafting class — it is
explicitly a **bond-focused** one, and his plan is not to get stronger but to reach specific
powerful people who, in the previous timeline, never cooperated with each other. The world ended
because egotists fought separately. His second run is spent making things for other people in order
to make them work together.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **The maker is the protagonist and the fighters are the supporting cast.** Equipment is the relationship.
2. **The apocalypse was a coordination failure**, not a power failure. Everyone was strong enough; nobody combined.
3. **A gift is a claim on someone.** Making something for a person is how the plot advances.
4. **Materials are the bottleneck**, so the social game is really a procurement game.
5. **The one who made the thing is usually not the one who used it.**

## Candidates

### `MK12` — are charms manufactured or found?
**Candidate:** **made by delvers, for delvers.** Every charm found on a body (`D24`) names a maker, and the maker is very probably also dead.
**Falls out:** a quiet inheritance chain nobody arranged — the thing that saved you was made for someone else, by someone who died. `BD2` (is a grace a physical, moral, or legal event) can then be answered as *someone made it happen, and they're gone*.
**Costs:** nothing in the engine. This is pure fiction over an existing item table, which makes it one of the cheapest strong candidates on the whole list.

### `EC8` / `MK7` — who sells the party its weapons, where does the steel come from?
**Candidate:** the smith is the **end of a supply chain the party is standing in**. They didn't get the steel; someone did, at a cost, from a place that is on the map and dangerous.
**Why it fits:** answers `MK8` (a material worth a war) as a live condition rather than a lore note, and gives `EC5` (trade over distance) something specific to move.

### `LP6` / `LP7` — what's the largest thing a gift can be, must every background carry one?
**Candidate:** **not every background carries a gift, and a gift always names its maker.** A blade at 90 gold is the ceiling, not the floor — anything larger stops being a piece of history and becomes a head start.
**Pairs with `MX23`:** yes, the player should see why they have it. "Made by X for Y, who didn't survive" is one line and does more than a stat.

### `OT8` — do the factions ally against the gates?
**Candidate:** **no, and that is the disaster.** Everyone is individually strong enough and nobody combines. The gates aren't winning on power.
**Why it fits:** answers `HS8` (where is the world going) without inventing a doom clock — the trajectory is bad for a reason that is entirely political.
**Costs:** commits `RU8` and `RU11` to a world where the factions' mutual hostility is the actual threat, which makes `GT15` (would shutting every gate be good) much sharper.

### `SM8` — how do we make a hire matter?
**Candidate (variant):** **you made their gear.** A companion carrying something you made, or something made for someone who died, is attachment without a single line of dialogue.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **The fandom coverage is thin.** There's no substantial wiki — the material is series blurbs, NovelUpdates tags and reader comments. Treat the *premise* as reliable (blacksmith, regression to Babel Academy, bond-focused crafting, coordination-failure apocalypse) and treat everything about the system's specifics as unverified.
- **Reader consensus is that the protagonist is thin** and the appeal is the craft-and-alliance loop rather than the character. That is convenient: this entry is a systems donor and should not be asked for tone.
- **Titles vary widely** across translations — *Can Make Them All*, *Makes Everything*, *Creates Everything*, *Will Make It All*. Note whichever you read; they're the same work.
- **The academy setting does not port.** Babel is a school, and Earth Kings has no institution that teaches delvers (`DV2` is still open). If you want the crafting candidates, `DV2` has to answer "there is a register but no school," or the academy arrives by the back door.

## What not to take

The regression. Foreknowledge is already spoken for — ORV's reader (`SF3`) and this source's
regressor are the same device from two angles, and the world only needs one entity that knows what
happens next. If `SF3` lands with the reader, this source contributes nothing above the forge.
