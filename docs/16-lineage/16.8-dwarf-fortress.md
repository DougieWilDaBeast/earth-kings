# 16.8 — Dwarf Fortress

**Tier B** · Bay 12 Games · **Serves:** `HS1`–`HS9`, `NW1`–`NW10`, `LW8`, `SM5`, `SM10`, `FR10`, `MK9`, `AR8`, `TG5`, `DV9`
**Checked against:** Dwarf Fortress Wiki (World generation, Legends, Advanced world generation)
The reference for **history that exists whether or not you play it**.

## What it is

Before play begins, the game runs centuries of history as, in Tarn Adams' own description, a
zero-player strategy game with thousands of agents and loose turn rules — and the history is simply
the log of that simulation. **Legends mode** then lets you read it: historical figures, sites,
structures, artifacts, wars, territorial maps you can scrub forwards and backwards through time.
Crucially, world generation can be set so **history is hidden** — the events all happened, but they
are only uncovered in play, by talking to people, reading books, finding artifacts, and looking at
engravings on coins and statues. Unimportant dead figures can be culled, after which the world
refers to them as "an unknown creature," and a surviving figure's parent can be recorded as *the
identity has been lost to time*.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Generate history, then hide it.** The world knows more than the player, and the difference is discoverable.
2. **Objects carry history.** An engraving, a coin, a statue is a delivery mechanism for a fact about the past.
3. **The record forgets unevenly.** Some people are culled to "unknown"; some parentages are simply lost.
4. **Grudges outlive their participants** and are the main driver of long-run politics.
5. **Your fortress is one entry among thousands.** The player's story is recorded in the same format as everyone else's.

## Candidates

### `HS8` — is the world's story going somewhere, and what if the player never existed?
**Candidate:** **the next fifty years happen regardless.** The run is one legible thread pulled through a world that was already moving, and it gets recorded in the same format as everything else.
**Falls out:** `SM5` gains its best supporting argument (the world persists because it was never about you), and `DV9` — the twelve are twelve among many, listed alongside people you never met.

### `HS3` / `HS5` / `TG5` — how far does reliable memory go, who's forgotten, how are events dated?
**Candidate:** memory forgets **unevenly, not gradually.** Some things are recorded precisely; some people are "an unknown"; some parentages are lost to time. Dating is by disaster and by ruler, and the two don't agree.
**Why it fits:** it gives `HS5` (one figure everyone's heard of, one nobody has) a *reason* rather than a pair of names, and it makes `NW5` (is there a central written record) answerable as "several, all partial."

### `MK9` / `AR8` — things made by a lost hand, and what a ruin is
**Candidate:** ruins are **legible**. A ruin says what it was, who held it, and roughly when it stopped, to anyone who can read a carving — and most people can't.
**Falls out:** `KN5` (literacy in a village) matters mechanically for the first time: the ruin has been telling everyone for two hundred years.

### `LW8` — is vengeance a recognised right?
**Candidate:** **yes, and no feud has ever been successfully ended.** The +10% grudge damage is the mechanical tip of a system where `annals.gd` keeps feuds alive longer than the people in them.
**Pairs with `LP10`–`LP12`:** a grudge against something that can't be fought is just a feud whose other side got culled.

### `FR10` — who tells this world's story to itself?
**Candidate:** **nobody, deliberately.** Things get written down — annals, memorials, engravings — but no one is assembling them. `annals.gd` is a pile, not a chronicle.
**Costs:** directly contradicts ORV's `SF3` reader candidate. These two cannot both be true; this is a divergence-ledger item, and probably the most interesting disagreement available to you and your brother.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **DF's myth-and-magic generator is long-announced and only partly delivered.** Don't build a candidate on procedurally generated cosmology — it isn't reliably in the shipped game, and Earth Kings' `PW1` needs one authored answer anyway.
- **Legends mode is a reading experience, not a play experience.** Most of DF's celebrated history is consumed *outside* the game by people who enjoy archives. `SM10` (threads are never shown) is the relevant warning: a history nobody reads did not happen for the player. Any candidate here needs a delivery surface — engraving, rumour, memorial — or it is decoration.
- **Culling is a performance optimisation** that produced a beloved fiction ("lost to time"). Worth remembering that the best flavour on this list is an accident of a database cleanup. Don't over-author what a constraint would give you for free.

## What not to take

Depth for its own sake. DF simulates things no player will ever observe, and it can afford to
because it is one man's life's work with no ship date. Earth Kings has a festival submission and
two people. Every candidate here has to justify itself against `SM12` — what mechanic would you cut
tomorrow.
