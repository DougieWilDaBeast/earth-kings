# 16.14 — That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime

**Co-founder's list** · Japanese light novel, Fuse · **Brief as given:** *landscape, cultures, religions, powers, standard of fantasy*
**Serves:** `LF1`–`LF8`, `BD1`–`BD5`, `NW7`, `MN1`–`MN5`, `OT1`–`OT9`, `RU3`–`RU9`, `LP1`–`LP7`, `KI9`, `SM8`, `MX19`
**Checked against:** Tensura Fandom (Name, The Octagram), Wikipedia character/location list

## What it is

A man dies and wakes as a slime in the Great Forest of Jura, and the series is mostly about
**founding a country**. The mechanism worth the whole entry is **naming**. In master–subordinate
naming, the namer spends magicules proportionate to the strength of the being named. The named gains
a powerful **soul bond** with the namer, may inherit some of their abilities, and — for monsters —
**evolves**. Name someone too strong, or too many without recovery, and the energy may **never fully
return**. There is a safer method that draws on ambient magicules instead, at the cost of a much
weaker bond. And the load-bearing part: monsters are **born incomplete**. An unnamed monster's soul
dissolves back into magicules when it dies. A named one persists.

Above ground level: eight Demon Lords, the **Octagram**, each with a numbered seat and a title, who
meet, argue, and occasionally renounce their seat to serve someone else. A sealed dragon's
disappearance created a power vacuum that reorganised an entire forest's politics without a battle.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Naming costs the namer, permanently if overdone.** Power given away is gone.
2. **A name completes a soul.** The unnamed dissolve; the named persist and can return.
3. **Cheap naming makes weak bonds.** There is a safe route and it is worse.
4. **A name can be nullified when the namer dies** — the bond was doing the work.
5. **The top of the power structure is a club with seats**, and seats are vacated by choice as often as by force.
6. **A power vacuum reorganises a region** more than a war does.

## Candidates

### `SM8` / `MX19` — how do we make a hire matter, and is a named death different?
**Candidate:** **you name them, and it costs you.** A recruit who takes a name from you gains standing, proficiency, or a grace — and you permanently give up something to do it. Names are the scarce resource, not gold.
**Why it fits:** it is the strongest available answer to `SM8` because it makes attachment a *mechanic the player chose to pay for*, and it resolves `MX19` cleanly — a named death is different because you can feel where the cost went.
**Against 16.5:** XCOM says the world names them for you, after the fact, from what they did. Tensura says you name them, in advance, at a price. These are genuinely different games and cannot both be true. **Primary ledger item.**
**Costs:** a naming economy is new systems work — a permanent resource the player spends on people. Not small, but it sits on top of the existing `Character`/`Unit` split rather than fighting it.

### `BD1` / `BD3` / `NW7` — what happens to the dead, and who is remembered?
**Candidate:** **the unnamed are not remembered because there is nothing left to remember.** A nameless recruit's death is a fact; a named one's death leaves a bond that other people can feel go.
**Falls out:** `memorials.json` gets a rule instead of a threshold — memorials are for the named, and that's why there are so few.
**Costs:** commits `BD2` (is a grace physical, moral or legal) to physical. That forecloses the ORV-flavoured reading where a grace is a story about you.

### `LF1` / `LF7` — what is a gate-thing, really?
**Candidate:** **incomplete.** Gate-things are not evil and not animals; they are unfinished, and the ones that get named on their own side stop being monsters.
**Falls out:** `OT4` (are the beasts considered people) — the Bamboo Court's beasts are the named ones. `OT7` (is a monster's death mourned) — only by the named ones, and there are some. This is the single best available answer to the `OT` block and it costs nothing in the engine.

### `RU4` / `RU8` / `RU9` — what the factions actually are
**Candidate:** the ten factions are a **club with seats**, not ten armies. They know each other, they meet, and the real events are seats changing hands — by abdication as often as by war.
**Why it fits:** `RU11` (is anyone winning) — no, because the seats are the point and nobody wants the table overturned. It also gives `OT8` (do they ally against the gates) the right shape: they'd have to admit the table doesn't cover it.

### `LP1` / `LP5` — what is a background, at root?
**Candidate:** a background is **who named you, and what it cost them.** That one line generates an origin, a debt and a person who is elsewhere.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **Naming rules are the most-argued mechanic in the fandom**, and the forum threads contradict each other on whether the safe method drains the *named*'s own magicules, whether re-naming stacks, and what happens when the namer dies. The canonical wiki entry is clear on the core (cost proportional to strength, risk of permanent loss, soul bond, evolution, weaker bond for the safe method) and vague beyond it. **Take only the core** — the vagueness is where the fandom argues, and Earth Kings gains nothing from resolving it.
- **A lot of high-traffic search results are for the Minecraft mod**, not the series. Its numbers (EP costs, percentages) are mod balance, not canon. Do not let a mod wiki set a design number.
- **The power-scaling half of this series is runaway.** Ultimate Skills, existence values in the hundreds of thousands, tiers above tiers. Earth Kings caps at proficiency 100 for a reason (`PW9`). Every candidate above is from the *first third* of the story, before scaling took over; that's deliberate.

## What not to take

Nation-building as the game. Tensura's back half is infrastructure, diplomacy and city management,
and it's the part most readers love. It is also a completely different genre from a tactics roguelite
and would answer `FR1` with something the build cannot deliver.
