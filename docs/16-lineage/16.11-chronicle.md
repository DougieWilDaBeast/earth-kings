# 16.11 — CHRONICLE (internal)

**Tier B, internal** · your own project · **Serves:** `SM3`, `SM5`, `MN8`, `MX11`, `MX13`, `HS8`, `PW1`, `PW12`, `SF6`, `CO8`
No fandom to research. This entry exists because it is the only donor on the list whose design
decisions you can actually change, and because it is already solving the hardest open question in
Earth Kings from a different direction.

## What it is

A medieval battlefield simulation where rules are **authored once** and executed by autonomous
units, with the ruleset evolving across generations. The player writes doctrine; the units carry it
out; what survives contact gets carried forward. The design-consultation work around it landed on
the same shape — a doctrine engine where the player authors rules that autonomous units execute
across generational strata — with award-winning precedents in Baba Is You, the Zachtronics
catalogue, Gladiabots and Dwarf Fortress.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Rules are the inheritance, not power.** What crosses a generation is *how things are done*, and it arrives degraded.
2. **The author is absent at execution.** You write the doctrine and then watch it be wrong.
3. **Generational strata make failure legible.** You can see which rule killed which cohort.
4. **A rule that survives is not a rule that was right** — it's one that nothing tested.
5. It is a **doctrine engine**, and Earth Kings already has a thing called doctrine.

## Candidates

### `MX13` — does anything carry between runs, and is that a betrayal of pillar 4?
**Candidate:** what carries is **rules, not power.** The next run inherits how the world behaves — which graces are common, what a town expects of a delver, whether feuds get settled — and inherits nothing you owned.
**Why it fits:** it is a defensible reading of pillar 4 rather than an exception to it. Nothing in the next run is easier; things are *differently shaped*.
**Costs:** it needs a persistence format the build doesn't have. `World` already serialises; a world-rule delta is new. Honest estimate: this is the most expensive candidate in the whole lineage set.

### `SM5` / `MX11` — is the next run the same world, later?
**Candidate:** **yes, and the mechanism is doctrine drift.** What the last run taught, spread and left behind is what the new run's world assumes.
**Against ORV's version:** ORV says the previous run survives as *text* (remembered, distorted). CHRONICLE says it survives as *behaviour* (nobody remembers, everyone does it). These are compatible and better together — the memory is wrong and the habit is right. If you take both, `SM6`'s Museum becomes the place where the two disagree in public.

### `KN1` / `KN9` — doctrine fading, and whether doctrine can be wrong
**Candidate:** doctrine fades because a rule nobody executes stops being a rule. `KN9` — **yes, there are false books**, and they persist precisely as long as nobody tests them.
**Why it fits:** it gives the 900-step fade a second, mechanical justification alongside WATF's epistemological one. Two independent readings landing on the same number is a good sign for the number.

### `MN8` — what should an NPC want by default?
**Candidate:** **to follow the rule they were taught**, including when it no longer fits. Not safety, not status — habit. That is a default an LLM-free NPC can actually execute, which matters because `D03` defers minds.

### `SF6` — does anyone understand the step-clock?
**Candidate:** **no, but some people have correct rules derived from it.** "Sharpen at dusk, never at dawn" is a step-clock observation nobody knows is one. That reframes `KN8`'s aphoristic doctrine style as *compressed empiricism*, which is a much better answer than "it's a genre."

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **The two projects want different things from a generation.** CHRONICLE's generations are the medium; Earth Kings' runs are a container for one story. If a candidate here requires the player to *author* rules, it belongs in CHRONICLE and not here — Earth Kings' player executes doctrine, they don't write it. That is the line, and it's worth writing into `BN`.
- **Verification instinct is a real bias here.** Testbench-shaped thinking — proving a rule correct, exhaustive coverage — fits CHRONICLE and quietly fights `FR8`, which demands something stay unexplained forever. Watch for candidates that want the Codex to be *completable in principle*.
- **Two engines, one person's attention.** The candidates above are the cheapest place to reuse thinking across both projects. `MX13` is the exception: it's expensive in both.

## What not to take

The autonomy. `SM3` asks whether `Q` — the AI playing itself — is anything in the fiction. CHRONICLE
makes autonomy the entire point, and the temptation to make Earth Kings' autoplay diegetic follows
directly from that. The safer answer for this project is that autoplay is **a tool and nothing**,
because the alternative commits you to explaining who is acting when you aren't.
