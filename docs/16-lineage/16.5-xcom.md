# 16.5 — XCOM

**Tier B** · Firaxis, 2012 / 2016 (and Julian Gollop's 1994 original) · **Serves:** `BD2`, `BD8`, `SM6`, `SM7`, `SM8`, `MX12`, `MX19`, `NW7`, `DV10`, `EC12`
The reference for **how permadeath is made to hurt** without the fiction doing any work.

## What it is

Squad-based turn tactics where soldiers are procedurally named nobodies who become specific people
through survival. They gain ranks, nicknames, and scars; they die to a 15% shot; and the game keeps
a **memorial wall** listing every one with their rank, nickname, kill count and the mission they
died on. The strategic layer above runs on scarcity — you cannot save everywhere, and the campaign
is a series of losses you chose.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **The nickname is the attachment mechanism.** A soldier becomes a person the moment the game gives them a second name it made up.
2. **The memorial does the emotional work the combat cannot.** The death is a dice roll; the wall is the story.
3. **Loss is surfaced later, unprompted, somewhere you went for another reason.**
4. **Scarcity is the narrative.** You are always choosing which thing to lose.
5. **Procedural identity beats authored identity at scale.** Nobody wrote Sgt. "Ratchet" Okonkwo, and that's why she's yours.

## Candidates

### `SM8` — how do we make a hire matter?
**Candidate:** **mechanics carry the name, fiction delivers it late.** A recruit earns a second name through what they did — proficiency, a grace they survived, a town they saved — and that name turns up afterwards in a place you went for another reason.
**Why it fits:** the repo already has `annals.gd`, `memorials.json`, `news.gd` and bond-tracked banter. This candidate requires no new system, only one link: the name must reach a surface the player visits for unrelated reasons.
**Costs:** commits `EC12` — companionship is a contract *and* the world writes it down anyway, which is the tension that makes dismissal feel bad.

### `MX19` — is a named hero's death different from a nameless recruit's?
**Candidate:** **no, and that's the point.** Both get the same line in the same annal. The difference is entirely how long you'd travelled with them, which is the player's problem, not the world's.
**Why it fits:** it protects `D14` (the run continues when companions die) from becoming a tier list of grief.

### `SM6` / `MX12` — what is the Museum?
**Candidate:** the memorial wall, **in the world, walkable**. A building you enter, with names, ranks, the deed that earned each one and the gate it ended at.
**Pairs with ORV's `SM5`:** if the next run is the same world later, the Museum is where the previous run is legible — and it is legible *wrongly*, because someone had to write it down.

### `BD8` — what do people do with bodies?
**Candidate:** the body goes wherever it fell; the **name** is what gets carried home and set down. `memorials.json` is for names, not remains.
**Falls out:** `NW7` (who remembers the dead, for how long) — as long as the memorial stands, which is longer than anyone who knew them.

### `SM7` — does danger survive free recovery?
**Candidate:** yes, because the resource that never restores is **people**. XCOM heals soldiers fully between missions and is still the most punishing game on this list. Full heal at a village is fine as long as the roster is the scarce thing.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **There is no fiction to research here.** XCOM's aliens are thin on purpose. This entry is a *mechanism* donor only — if a candidate from this file has any lore content in it, it came from somewhere else and should be re-sourced.
- **The two XCOMs disagree about the strategic layer.** XCOM 2's timer pressure produces very different play from XCOM 1's. Earth Kings' step clock is closer to XCOM 2 (everything costs time), so where they differ, take 2.
- **The original 1994 game has permadeath without nicknames** and is measurably less affecting. That's the cleanest evidence for the candidate above: the naming, not the dying, is the mechanism.

## What not to take

The barracks screen. XCOM's roster UI is a management surface, and `MX14` / `SF4` are already
worried about how much the interface knows that the character doesn't. Take the memorial, leave the
spreadsheet.
