# 16.6 — Baldur's Gate 3

**Tier B** · Larian, 2023 · **Serves:** `MX17`, `MX18`, `LP19`, `LP23`, `CH5`, `CH9`, `CH12`, `SM1`, `SM8`, `LP27`
The reference for **companions who are people rather than slots**, and for choices that close doors.

## What it is

A party RPG where every companion has a private agenda, an approval track, and a point at which
they will leave, betray, or refuse. Act-one decisions silently determine which act-three endings
exist. Save-scumming is possible and the game is designed around people doing it anyway. The
companions are not recruitable resources — several of them can be permanently lost by being who
you are in front of them.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **A companion's refusal is the character.** The moment they say no is the moment they stop being a stat line.
2. **Early choices are load-bearing much later**, and the game does not tell you which ones were.
3. **Approval is tracked but never shown as a number in-world.** You learn it from behaviour.
4. **The party talks to itself.** Banter between two companions is where relationships actually live.
5. **Losing a companion is a real loss of content**, not a rebalance.

## Candidates

### `MX18` — should a named hero refuse to join based on creed, or does gold always work?
**Candidate:** **refusal, and gold never moves them.** The sixteen are not hires; the fifteen you didn't become have opinions about you, and at least three of them are wrong about you in interesting ways.
**Falls out:** `MX17` (how hiring works) — a price for nameless recruits, a **condition** for named ones. `EC10` (is there anything money can't buy) gets a concrete instance instead of a philosophical one.
**Costs:** real work. Each of the sixteen needs a `CH5` answer (what they refuse to do) before this can ship, which front-loads Part VI.

### `LP19` / `LP23` — can a creed be a lie, can an oath break in play?
**Candidate:** **yes to both, and the break is visible to the party before it's visible to the player.** A character whose creed and conduct diverge gets banter about it from someone who noticed.
**Why it fits:** the repo has bond-tracked banter with warm/cold thresholds already. This is a content answer, not an engine answer.

### `CH9` — who would they not travel with?
**Candidate:** make the refusal **asymmetric**. A won't travel with B; B has no idea and likes A. That single asymmetry generates more character than a mutual grudge.
**Pairs with `LP22`:** an oath can name someone who never knew.

### `SM1` — is save-scumming a fact about the world, the player, or a hole?
**Candidate:** **a fact about the player, declared openly.** BG3 permits it, expects it, and never fictionalises it — and the game is not weaker for the honesty.
**Why it fits:** it lets `CO7` (revisit D21) resolve as *keep it, stop apologising*, and keeps `SM5` free to be answered by the world rather than by the save system.
**Costs:** if ORV's `SM5` candidate lands (next run is the same world later), this one gets harder — reloading and re-running become different kinds of repetition, and `MX13` has to say which one carries.

### `LP27` — does the player ever see a character's full history?
**Candidate:** **no.** You learn a companion by travelling with them, and some of it only if you're there at the right moment. Anything on a party screen is what they'd tell a stranger.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **BG3's scale is unreproducible.** Its companion depth is thousands of lines of voiced, branching dialogue. Earth Kings defers minds entirely (`D03`) and has no voice budget. Every candidate here must survive being delivered as **banter lines and refusals**, not as scenes. Two of the five above already do; `LP19` is the one at risk.
- **Larian's approval system is contested** among players as either the best or the crudest part of the design. Take the *refusal threshold*, not the running score — the score is the part that turns companions into slot machines.
- **Act-three endings gated by act-one choices** is widely praised and also the source of most player frustration. For Earth Kings, `SM5` decides whether this is even possible: irreversible gating only means something if the world persists.

## What not to take

Romance. It is the single largest driver of BG3's companion engagement and it is not what this
project is for — it would land on `BN4` (subject matter off the table) before it landed anywhere
useful, and the sixteen tempers plus romance is a dating sim wearing a tactics game.
