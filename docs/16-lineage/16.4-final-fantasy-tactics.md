# 16.4 — Final Fantasy Tactics / Tactics Advance

**Tier B** · Square, 1997 / 2003 · **Serves:** `RU1`–`RU13`, `LW1`–`LW4`, `FR1`, `FR7`, `DV4`, `SM12`, `KI6`, `HS7`
The stated primary reference for the project, and the one this world's *politics* should come from.

## What it is

A succession war fought in the gap between two noble houses, told from the side of the man history
credits to someone else. Ramza and Delita start as friends of unequal birth; Delita climbs by using
the church and the war, Ramza is written out as a heretic. The player never gets a battle that
means nothing — every fight is a faction position, and units defect, are recruited, are lost
permanently. FFTA inverts it: a boy wishes the world into a fantasy where nobody can be hurt, and
the game is about whether that's a crime.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Politics is legible through battle composition.** Who stands next to whom on a map tells you the whole alliance state without a codex entry.
2. **The side arc reframes the main one.** Delita's story runs alongside Ramza's and retroactively changes what Ramza's was about.
3. **The official history is wrong, and the game knows.** The framing device is a scholar reading records that disagree with what you played.
4. **Class is the theme, not the mechanic.** Job flexibility sits inside a story about birth deciding everything.
5. **FFTA:** the antagonist's position is defensible. A world without loss is the thing the protagonist destroys.

## Candidates

### `RU1` — nine of ten factions hostile: real politics, or the view from the road?
**Candidate:** **the view from the road**. The Heart Empire's line infantry and a Freehold militia are the same people two years apart, and a delving party is a small armed group that nobody asked for.
**Falls out:** `DV4` — dread, then business. `RU10` (who protects a village) — whoever is closest and cheapest, which is why the Freeholds aren't hostile: they can't afford to be.
**Costs:** this makes `LW2` (raiding a town) unambiguously a crime rather than frontier normality.

### `RU2` — why did the Heart Empire come up the road?
**Candidate:** a **succession**, and the reason is embarrassing. Someone needed a war to be legitimate, and nobody at the sharp end was told.
**Why it fits:** answers `RU2`'s second half — *do they know it themselves* — with "the men on the road don't, and the three people who do are dead or lying." Sets up `RU4` (is there a throne, is anyone on it) as: there is a throne, and the question of who sits on it is exactly what started this.

### `FR7` — what should the player feel ten seconds after losing someone?
**Candidate:** **the flat click of a rule first.** Grief arrives late, somewhere else — a name in a list, a memorial you walked past for another reason.
**Why it fits:** it's the honest description of what permadeath actually does, and it tells `SM8` what its job is: mechanics carry the name, fiction delivers it late.

### `HS7` / `KI6` — one war or many, and is the rootless adventurer the norm?
**Candidate:** **one war**, and the twelve are survivorship bias. Dead forge-masters, raided borderlands and a monastery the war crept past are three views of the same twenty years.
**Falls out:** `HS1` (biggest thing in the past) and `HS6` (last thing that changed the world) collapse into one answer, which is cheap and strong.

### `FR1` — what is the world about?
**Candidate:** the FFT answer is *the record is written by the winner and the record is wrong*. That is compatible with the repo's `annals.gd` and with `SF3` — but only one of them can be the frame. Worth marking against ORV's reader.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **FFT and FFTA want opposite things.** FFT says loss is the point; FFTA says a world without loss is a lie worth destroying. They agree on the conclusion and disagree on the tone. Earth Kings' `SM2` (difficulty settings that remove permadeath) sits exactly on that seam — take FFT for `BN`/`FR`, take FFTA only for `SM2`/`MX22`, and never mix them in one sitting.
- **The 1997 translation is notoriously unreliable** and *War of the Lions* rewrote it in archaic register. If you're quoting FFT as a tone reference, name which version — they do not read the same, and the archaic one is closer to what the Question Book's own prose is already doing.

## What not to take

The named-lord roster. FFT's politics works because there are about eight people who matter and
you learn all their names. Earth Kings has ten factions and no NPC system yet (`D03` defers minds).
Importing named political actors writes a cheque `MN8` and `SM11` haven't agreed to.
