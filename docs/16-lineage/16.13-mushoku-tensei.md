# 16.13 — Mushoku Tensei (Jobless Reincarnation)

**Co-founder's list** · Japanese light novel, Rifujin na Magonote · **Brief as given:** *landscape, cultures, religions, powers, standard of fantasy*
**Serves:** `LN1`–`LN10`, `CL5`, `SK9`, `FA1`–`FA10`, `TG1`–`TG5`, `RU3`, `PW5`, `PW11`, `KN4`, `AR2`, `EC5`, `OT1`–`OT4`, `DV5`
**Checked against:** Mushoku Tensei Fandom (World Map, Central Continent, Millis Continent, Demon Continent, Timeline)

## What it is

The most thoroughly built "standard fantasy" world in modern isekai, and the reason to study it is
that almost nothing in it is arbitrary. Five continents, each with a **different mana density** —
the Demon Continent's is so high that every monster there is D-rank or above and they adapt to a
technique after seeing it once, which is why it's the most dangerous place in the world, not because
the author said so. The Millis Continent is split by the Blue Dragon Mountain Range, with the **Holy
Sword Highway** cut straight through it — a road with **not a single monster on it**, carved by a
god during a war. The Millis religion is a Catholic analogue headquartered in the same city as the
**Adventurer's Guild** headquarters. Magic runs on seven ranks: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced,
Saint, King, Emperor, God, and the rank is set by *scale and complexity of manipulation*, not by
which spell you know. Above that sit **Seven Great Powers** — fixed titles, of which the lower three
can be claimed by defeating the current holder and the upper four have not changed in centuries.
Teleportation magic exists and is largely forbidden. The Central and Demon Continents are the two
halves of a single older continent, split by a mana calamity at the end of a war.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **Mana density is a geographic variable.** Danger is a property of *place*, and it explains the monsters, the politics and the trade routes at once.
2. **A safe road is a historical artifact.** The Holy Sword Highway is a war measure that outlived the war and now structures all commerce.
3. **Rank means scale, not spell list.** A Saint-tier caster does bigger weather, not different weather.
4. **Titles are held, and the lower ones are takeable.** The hierarchy has a mechanism for change and mostly doesn't use it.
5. **The map's shape is a scar.** Two continents exist because one broke.
6. **Church HQ and guild HQ share a city**, which is how institutions actually behave.

## Candidates

### `LN1` / `CL5` / `OT2` — why is one region more dangerous than another?
**Candidate:** **density of the grammar varies by place.** Where it's thick, gate-things are stronger, doctrine is easier and stranger, and people live differently. Where it's thin, life is ordinary and delving is a story.
**Falls out:** more than any other candidate in either list — `LF6` (why beasts cluster), `EC5` (why trade goes the long way), `DV5` (why delvers concentrate), `AR2` (why some villages are walled), `KI3` (why people move). One variable, six answers.
**Costs:** it has to be *visible on the node map* or it's a note nobody reads. That's map work, not fiction work.

### `LN5` / `EC5` — what does the road network look like?
**Candidate:** one road is **safe because of something that happened**, and everything expensive travels on it. The others are cheaper and kill people.
**Why it fits:** it turns the FFTA-style node map from a menu into a political object, and it gives `HS6` (the last thing that changed the world) a physical consequence you can stand on.

### `PW5` / `PW11` — what separates a novice from a master?
**Candidate:** **scale, not vocabulary.** The same doctrine at proficiency 10 and proficiency 90 is the same act done bigger. Nobody learns a secret ninth technique.
**Why it fits:** it's consistent with a proficiency number the repo already has, and it kills the temptation to gate content behind hidden skills.

### `FA1` / `FA3` / `RU3` — is there a church, and what does it want?
**Candidate:** **one large faith with a headquarters, a highway and an army**, plus local practice everywhere that doesn't match it. The faith is the second-largest political actor on the map and does not think of itself as political.
**Falls out:** `FA5` (do the faithful and the delvers disagree) — constantly, and it's about the gates. `KN4` (who can read) — the church can, which is why the church has the history.

### `TG1` / `TG3` — how many languages?
**Candidate:** **three that matter**, split by race and continent, plus one dead one that doctrine is written in. A character who can read the dead one is a character with a past.

### `AR2` — walls
**Candidate (from the Heaven Continent):** at least one settlement is protected by **wards instead of walls**, because the thing it fears comes from above. One detail, and it tells you the whole region.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **The "Six-Faced World" cosmology is late, thin and contested** among readers — the cube-shaped world and the god summit are not well integrated with the ground-level worldbuilding. Take the continents and the mana density; leave the cosmology entirely. Earth Kings' `FR8` is better served by not having one.
- **Magic ranks are listed as Emperor on some sources and Imperial on others**, and the Seven Great Powers roster shifts with the plot. Take the *principle* (seven tiers, scale-defined; a small number of named titles, the lower ones contestable) and don't reproduce the ladder.
- **The source's reputation is complicated.** The worldbuilding is widely admired and the protagonist's early conduct is widely and legitimately criticised. That's easy to handle here: this is a geography and institutions donor, and none of the candidates above touch a character.

## What not to take

Reincarnation and the meta-plot. The Man-God, the loops, the hidden puppeteer — Earth Kings already
has two candidates for "someone knows what happens next" (**16.2**, **16.10**) and does not need a
third. Take the map and the church.
