# 16.16 — Solo Leveling

**Co-founder's list** · Korean web novel / manhwa, Chugong · **Brief as given:** *gates, skills, monsters*
**Serves:** `GT1`–`GT8`, `GT15`, `LF2`, `LF5`, `DV1`–`DV6`, `EC1`–`EC9`, `RU10`, `LW6`, `NW4`, `OT8`, `MX5`, `MX9`
**Checked against:** Solo Leveling Fandom (Gates, Dungeons, Magic Beasts, Terminologies)

## What it is

The most mechanically explicit gate world in the genre, and therefore the most useful to argue with.
**Gates** are portals that appear anywhere at random — highways, abandoned lots, schools. They stay
open until the **boss inside is defeated**. If a gate isn't cleared within roughly a week, a
**Dungeon Break** occurs and everything inside walks out into the world. Gates, dungeons, monsters
and hunters all share one **E-to-S rank scale**, measured by mana output on a sensor; **higher-rank
gates are visibly larger** — low ones look like doors, an S-rank looks like a hurricane. Hunters are
ordinary people who **awakened**, are measured by a meter, sort into subtypes (fighter, mage, healer,
ranger, assassin, tank), and make a living clearing dungeons and harvesting **mana crystals**. Weak
hunters get sent in to collect loot while strong ones fight. Guilds carry real political and economic
weight. In the revised timeline, an unattended break **contaminates the land permanently** and makes
it uninhabitable.

## Mechanisms worth taking

1. **The gate has a clock.** Ignoring it is a choice with a consequence, so every open gate is pressure.
2. **Danger is measurable, and visibly so.** You can see how bad it is before you go in, and a machine agrees.
3. **One scale for everything.** Gate, monster, hunter and reward all read on the same letter.
4. **Delving is employment with a class structure.** Some hunters fight; some carry bags; the bag-carriers get paid worse and die anyway.
5. **Failure damages the world, not the player.** A break costs civilians.

## Candidates

### `GT7` / `GT15` / `OT8` — what happens if a gate is left alone?
**Candidate:** **it spills.** An open gate left long enough starts pushing outward — beasts on the roads, then a settlement gone, then land that nobody farms again. The map gets worse while you're elsewhere.
**Why it fits:** the repo has a step clock, a world that moves between visits, and gates that open and close. This connects all three into pressure, and it answers `GT15` (would shutting every gate be good) with a hard **yes** — which is a cleaner frame than ambiguity.
**Costs:** it commits the game to a *losing* world state, which collides with `SM2` and `MX22` (difficulty, and whether the world can become unwinnable). Also collides with **16.3**'s reading of gates as ordinary workplaces — a thing with a doom clock is not a job. **Ledger item**, and the most consequential one in the co-founder's list.

### `GT2` / `GT4` — can you tell how bad a gate is before entering?
**Candidate:** **yes, by looking.** Size, colour, sound. No sensor, no number — the world tells you honestly and people go in anyway because of what's on the other side.
**Why it fits:** it gives the node map information the player can act on without a UI element, and it makes `DV3` (how a party chooses a gate) a real decision rather than a menu.
**Against Solo Leveling:** the source uses a measuring device and an association that dispatches teams. Earth Kings has no such institution and shouldn't invent one — replacing the meter with the eye is the port.

### `EC1` / `EC2` / `EC7` — what is the delving economy?
**Candidate:** **the gate is the only source of a material the world now depends on.** Not treasure — an input. That's why a dangerous job has a queue, and why towns tolerate armed strangers (`DV4`).
**Falls out:** `EC9` (does gate-gold wreck prices) — yes, locally and visibly, and the towns nearest the open gates are rich and unpleasant.

### `DV5` / `LW6` — who is allowed to delve?
**Candidate:** **anyone, and that's the problem.** No licence, no association, no dispatch. People who shouldn't go, go, and the parties that take them in as bag-carriers are doing something the world hasn't decided is a crime.
**Why it fits:** it answers `LW6` (what the law doesn't cover) with the thing the player is doing, which is far better than an abstract gap.

### `LF2` / `LF5` — do the beasts get stronger, and do they organise?
**Candidate:** **they get stronger where the gate is older.** Time open, not player level. A gate nobody has touched in two years is worse than a fresh one — and the world scales by neglect rather than by you.
**Why it fits:** it's the cleanest anti-level-scaling answer available, and it makes exploration a race rather than a progression curve.

## Ambiguity, and how to resolve it

- **There are two timelines.** The manhwa/novel revise history mid-story, and the permanent mana-contamination rule, the Rulers-and-Monarchs backstory, and several gate facts belong to the revised one. Fan sources mix them freely. If a candidate cites *why gates appeared*, it's from the revised timeline and it's the weakest material here — Earth Kings' `GT5` should not be answered from this source.
- **The System is a special case, not world physics.** Jinwoo's levelling is granted to him alone by an entity. Anything in this source that reads like "how progression works" is a plot device for one character. Take gate mechanics and hunter economics; take nothing about skills.
- **The modern-world setting does most of the work.** Gates are frightening partly because they appear on a highway. Earth Kings is pre-modern, so the contrast is unavailable — the substitute is that the gates are *newer than the settlements*, which is worth answering at `GT5`/`HS6`.

## What not to take

The rank letters. E-to-S on everything is the genre's default and it is exactly what `BN6` (what
you're afraid this becomes) is watching for. Earth Kings already prices gates in floors and
characters in proficiency; a letter grade over the top would be the moment the world stops being
Earth Kings.
