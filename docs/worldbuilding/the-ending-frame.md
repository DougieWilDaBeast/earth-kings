# The ending frame: Pangaea

Written 2026-10-24 from `dougie`'s message of that morning
([transcript](voice-notes/2026-10-24-dougie-lost-places-and-pangaea.md), [D440](../06-decisions.md),
issue #568). **Direction, not the build.** Nothing here is in the game, and nothing is built before
[M17](../05-roadmap.md#m17--past-the-top--design-first) has a design both founders sign off;
code waits on play-test 9 in any case ([D383](../06-decisions.md)).

What is `dougie`'s word is marked as his and is canon. What the writers and designers read into it,
to make it fit what exists, is written **Inferred.** and is on the agenda (rows 340 to 342) and in
[30](../30-questions-for-the-founders.md) as questions 49 to 54, each with a default.

## What `dougie` said

- **The worlds are not copies.** Everyone, the player included, believes the sixteen worlds are
  copies of one ([D31](../06-decisions.md)). In fact "they would in fact be more together than we
  think": they are one land, broken apart, the way Pangaea was the one connected landmass before
  the continents.
- **Floor 100 remerges them.** When the player reaches floor 100 "and then they manage to beat the
  bosses and/or what they think is the final level, the worlds are remerged together", and "all the
  separate stories that occurred get rolled into one".
- **The fallen are real, and they know.** The leads whose worlds were destroyed "are still real and
  they are now demon kings as we know", but with "all the knowledge of who they were in the parallel
  universes too. They are aware that they are a demon king and they have no power to change, a true
  tragedy."
- **The living are merged too.** Every main character still alive because the player never picked
  them is merged as well, aware of their parallel lives: "in a sense it completes their
  understanding of their characters too."

the crossing and the census all stay as they are; the frame is what they turn out to mean.

## What it unlocks

- **Every death pays off at the end.** The more leads the player lost, the more demon kings stand in
  the merged land, each knowing who they were. A player who lost one has one tragedy; a player who
  lost fifteen faces almost the whole cast.
- **Relationships between the sixteen land here.** `dougie`, 2026-10-17: "if all 16 survive, they'll
  understand exactly what their relationships were with everyone. But maybe they could be upset
  after learning about certain people they had a relationship with and then they didn't make it to
  the end" ([transcript](voice-notes/2026-10-17-dougie-other-games.md), [D223](../06-decisions.md)).
  The merge is the moment they understand.
- **No two endings are the same.** Who fell, how far they got and who the player walked beside
  decide who is a demon king and what each remembers.
- **The ghost and the demon king are one person.** Trust earned at the fire comes back across a
  battlefield, from someone who remembers earning it.
- **The last act has a setting.** The one land is where the sixteen (living and fallen) turn on
  whoever broke it, or on each other.
- **A name the register already holds.** **Inferred.** The one land before the split is called
  Pangaea in the world too: Greek, "all the earth", beside the sixteen's Roman and Greek names and
  the game's title (question 54). The word is never said before floor 100.

## What it does not settle

Who or what broke the land, and why (question 46.3); who rules the one land, and whether "Earth King"
still means the contest's winner; what becomes of the people of each world (sixteen innkeepers of the
same inn, or one); whether the Forgotten Order, unaged in a Tower that is frozen in time
([D293](../06-decisions.md)), were ever split at all; and whether a demon king can be laid to rest
once beaten, if never redeemed. These are for the founders when M17's design is written; the six
that shape it now are questions 49 to 54 in [30](../30-questions-for-the-founders.md).

## The Forgotten Order's six names

Settled by `dougie` the same morning ([D429](../06-decisions.md), question 44): **Orion**,
**Atalanta** and **Castor** stay; the other three are **names of ancient places lost to time**,
"Atlantis, Babylon level". The writers drew a list (Babylon, Atlantis, Ur, Nineveh, Akkad, Uruk,
Carthage, Troy, Thule, Lemuria, Mohenjo-daro, Hyperborea) and picked the three that read best beside
the kept three, each keeping the fable's by-name of the one it replaces:

1. **Babylon, the Last Out of the Fire** (was Aeneas): led them in; the fables say he carried the other
   five out of a burning world first.
2. **Orion, the Hunter Who Climbs**: went ahead on every floor and came back to say what waited.
3. **Atalanta, the Runner**: never lost a race on the land, and in the Tower ran messages between
   floors nobody else could reach.
4. **Castor, the Twin Who Stayed**: his brother turned back at the door; the tales argue about which of
   them was the brave one.
5. **Carthage, the Spear Queen** (was Penthesilea): the one the land's monuments show, spear down,
   facing the Tower.
6. **Ur, the Mender** (was Asclepius): kept the six alive for a hundred years; some say he is why none
   of them has aged.

**Inferred** (agenda 327): the three picks and their by-names. Alternates, for `dougie` to swap in:
**Atlantis** (the one he named first, left out only because it sits close to Atalanta on the ear)
and **Nineveh**. `doug-md` may still change any. The climbers' stone keeps "six names worn too smooth
to read" ([D266](../06-decisions.md)) until the founders want them cut in; that is a change to
`data/world_rules.json` for after play-test 9.
